A cache stores recently used data in fast memory so later lookups avoid a slow backend. Its size is bounded, so when the cache is full some entry must be evicted. An eviction policy decides which entry to remove. LRU evicts the least recently used entry and is simple to reason about. A cache hit returns data quickly, while a miss pays the full backend latency. Too many misses, or a thrashing working set, can make the cache useless. Monitoring the hit rate guides tuning of capacity and policy.
