A message queue decouples producers from consumers by buffering messages between them. A producer appends a message to the queue, and a consumer reads it later, possibly at a different rate. Delivery ordering and acknowledgement policies matter: at-least-once delivery may redeliver a message after a crash. Consumers can read from multiple partitions to scale throughput, but ordering is only guaranteed within a single queue. Backpressure appears when producers outpace consumers and the queue fills. Observing consumer lag helps operators keep delivery healthy.
